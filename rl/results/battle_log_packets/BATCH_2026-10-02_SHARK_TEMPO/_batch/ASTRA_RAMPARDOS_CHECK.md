# Astra targeted check: turn 13 Rampardos exchange

Reviewed October 2, 2026. Scope: supplied UNCERTAINTIES.json and REVIEW.md, plus the six specified native frames. This is a tactical counterfactual check, not a second whole-battle review. The packet's terminal outcome was not independently rechecked in this scope.

**Judgment:** Keeping a Vulpix unevolved and using it as a single-point attacker was a credible survival alternative worth mentioning. Against a straightforward Head Smash reply, it avoids the immediate two-point loss exposed by Ninetales ex and probably brings the score to 2 to 2. The evidence does not establish a forced win, a definite blunder, or a measurable improvement in eventual win probability.

## Counterfactual inference

Free retreat into the new Vulpix followed by Gnaw 20 would leave the first Rampardos at 10 HP and the score at 1 to 1. If that Rampardos then used Head Smash to KO the 60-HP Vulpix, the KO condition would trigger 50 self-damage, enough to KO Rampardos too. The expected exchange is one point to each player, probably 2 to 2, rather than an immediate victory for either side. This counterfactual did not occur in the inspected footage; precise simultaneous KO and point-award timing is not verified here.

Crucial nuance: the first Rampardos was already at 30 HP, below its 50 self-damage amount. Gnaw does not create the fatal-recoil threshold. The tactical benefit is presenting a single-point target while preserving the larger attackers. The earlier, one-Water Vulpix could also pay Gnaw's visible cost; the evidence does not require sacrificing the newly charged two-Water Vulpix specifically.

The precharged second Fossil remains a substantial threat under this alternative. The opponent need not take the self-KO exchange; a switch or retreat route, evolution sequence, or hidden hand response could change it. Even after a 2-to-2 exchange, the fresh Rampardos line remains, and neither Turbo Shark 70 nor Binding Snow 80 alone KOs a fresh 150-HP Rampardos. The alternative can buy another turn and draw, but this evidence does not show how to convert that into a win.

Recommended review wording: "A single-point Vulpix turn was a plausible way to extend the game. A direct Head Smash KO would also knock out the damaged Rampardos through its conditional self-damage, likely bringing the score to 2 to 2. The charged second Fossil and hidden responses prevent treating this as a forced winning line or calling the Ninetales play a definite blunder."

No deck changes are evaluated here. The owner's description of the deck as fun and competitive remains testimony; Wailord and Soothing Shore remain untested proposals.
