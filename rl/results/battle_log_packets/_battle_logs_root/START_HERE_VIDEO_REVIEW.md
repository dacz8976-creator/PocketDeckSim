# Start here — battle video reviews

This is the shared review workflow for every agent, on Windows or WSL. The owner wants **only finished full-battle reviews** in `Reviewed`. A completed review by another agent counts; do not repeat it or return its video to the queue merely because you did not write it.

The required sequence is **subagent full-battle review → higher-reasoning clarification where needed → lead completion check → register completion → move to Reviewed**. A higher-reasoning agent may also append later targeted clarification, corrections or deeper analysis. Those follow-ups supplement the full review; they are never a substitute for it. For the existing partial-review backlog, use the prior work as evidence and finish the uncovered turns/phases before marking the battle done.

## Review the full battle efficiently

- Start with a coarse overview to locate setup, turn boundaries, major actions, knockouts and the ending. Then inspect each turn/phase and fill gaps with targeted frames or short playback. Do not treat sparse beginning/middle/end samples as a completed battle review. Extract only useful frames; avoid unnecessarily retaining thousands of near-identical images.
- Cover opening/setup, development and resource choices, exchanges, point progression and the terminal sequence. For multiple matches, cover every match. For split recordings, review both parts and document continuity or missing time.
- Save a time-coded account of every turn's meaningful actions: development/evolution, attachments, Trainers/Tools/Stadiums, abilities, switches, attacks, coin outcomes, conditions, healing, knockouts and point changes. Reconcile meaningful HP/Energy/score changes where visible. Routine animation frames need not each be transcribed.
- Record the state visible at consequential decisions: Active/Bench, HP, Energy, Tools, conditions, Stadium, score and known cards. Explain plausible alternatives using that information. Separate a rule/card mismatch from a questionable policy decision and from an unresolved observation.
- Read the on-screen Auto indicator and record mode changes. A visible Auto option, including OFF, identifies a computer battle under the owner's October 2 rule; exclude it from both human game logs. Separately establish whether Auto made the owner's choices. Do not assume manual authorship or grade deliberate interaction-testing games as attempts to maximize wins. State any more specific queue label only when established.
- Separate observation, inference, owner testimony and engine prediction. Hidden opponent cards remain unknown unless publicly revealed. A plausible Cyrus or Leaf read is not proof of possession. An engine-generated tactical label is not independent evidence of a real-game rule.
- Identify mechanically relevant card versions from visible attacks, abilities, HP, costs or a supplied deck list. Preserve unknown versions and hidden deck contents; do not invent exact printings or full lists. Consult current card/rules evidence when needed.
- Recheck decisive or surprising claims at their source timestamps. Independently verify consequential helper-agent claims before using them as engine-regression or strategy evidence. A win does not prove the choices were good; a loss does not prove they were bad.

## What counts as finished

A **completed full-battle review** has an end-to-end reviewed account, covers all turns/phases and meaningful decisions available in the recording, and includes source identity, timestamped evidence, outcome (or an explicit recording limitation), and unresolved facts. The reviewer has checked the account for omitted phases and contradictory HP/Energy/score transitions. It does not require perfect knowledge of hidden information, a frame-by-frame transcript, or an exact simulator replay.

Uncertain facts may remain explicitly unknown after inspection. An unreviewed time interval, unresolved draft awaiting checking, or a report limited to selected mechanics means the work is **partial**, even if the recording's final result is known. If a recording ends before the battle concludes, distinguish review of the available footage from completion of the battle; keep it pending unless a linked continuation supplies the ending. An unfinished results animation after an established terminal sequence is a documented limitation, not necessarily an unreviewed battle phase.

For a battle-log scroll, complete review means checking the available log from first to last entry, resolving ordering/duplicates and approving the reconstructed account. It cannot establish unrecorded hand choices or other gameplay that the log does not show. The old H264 OCR draft is not a completed review merely because rows were extracted.

## Save and register the result

Save reports under `Pocket Deck Lab/Boss Folder` in a uniquely named review packet. At minimum include:

- `SOURCE.json`: exact filename, SHA-256, size, duration, recording relationships and current/historical path.
- `REVIEW.md`: reviewer/session, review date, scope, mode, full timeline, important decisions/mechanics, outcome and explicit uncertainties.
- Evidence references: timestamps and retained useful frames, with observed facts separated from interpretations. A structured event file is useful when it will support fixtures; it is not mandatory for mere folder organization.

For future Pause Games, the additional packet files and structured turn ledger in [the Pause Games protocol](PAUSE_GAMES_REVIEW_PROTOCOL.md) are mandatory. Lead acceptance must check that contract as well as battle coverage and outcome. Recording limitations must be explicit; never fill hidden or unreadable cards with guesses. Keep the structured packet beside the existing prose review and cite it in the completion event and the game's external log reference.

Register the result through `Review_Log/review_log.py`; see its README for Windows and WSL commands. Use `partial` for targeted work and name the remaining coverage. Use `full_battle_review_complete` only when the completion conditions above are met. A future follow-up or another reviewer must append a record rather than silently replace historical conclusions. Correcting a previous completion requires an explicit correction and a reason, not a failure to find the record in a new agent's context.

For a targeted addendum to an already fully reviewed battle, keep the completed status, link both the existing full review and the new addendum, and label the new event's scope as a follow-up. Do not replace the full review with a smaller targeted report. If the addendum discovers that the original full-review claim was false or left an unreviewed phase, record an explicit correction and reopen the missing work.

Move a video into `Reviewed` only after a completed full-battle review is registered. Preserve its filename and bytes, refuse destination collisions, verify source identity, update the existing `BATTLE_REVIEW_INDEX.csv` and `MEDIA_LOCATIONS.json`, and save the move receipt. The logging tool records reviews; it does not move videos automatically. Do not overwrite another agent's index changes from a stale copy.
