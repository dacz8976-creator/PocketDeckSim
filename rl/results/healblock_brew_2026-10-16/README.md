# Claydol (Heal Block) decks: three ideas

Claydol's Heal Block stops healing for BOTH players. Each deck below has no healing cards of its own, so the
block only costs us in one way: damage on our own Pokemon (Hoopa ex's self-hit, attack recoil) stays put.
Claydol is a 100 HP Bench body in all three, and its attack is never the plan. Its Colorless costs work with any
Energy type. One bonus: Jolteon ex is weak to Fighting, so Baltoy (30+20) and Claydol (50+20) hit it harder.
Each deck has a `.txt` list, a `.png` QR and a code (code in `checks.txt`, which also has the decode check).

## Deck 1: Weezing Smog (Darkness)  `list1_weezing_smog`
2 Team Rocket's Koffing, 2 Team Rocket's Weezing ex, 2 Baltoy, 1 Claydol, 1 Nihilego, 2 Hoopa ex,
1 Darkrai ex, 2 Poke Ball, 2 Professor's Research, 2 Deceptive Needle, 2 Cyrus, 1 Rocky Helmet

**Plan.** Turn 1: Koffing (or Hoopa ex) up front, Baltoy/Nihilego on the Bench. Turn 2: Hoopa ex hits for 30
and pings a Benched Pokemon for 20, Deceptive Needle adds 10 each turn. Turn 2-3: evolve Koffing into
Weezing ex from hand: Boiler Smog Poisons AND Burns their Active (10 + 20 per Checkup; Nihilego on the Bench
adds +10 to Poison, so 40 per Checkup until the Burn flips off). Claydol comes down on turn 2-3 (Baltoy, then
Claydol) so their Lucky Ice Pop / Potion / Lady can't undo the damage. Cyrus can drag up a Pokemon that
Hoopa pinged, and you can evolve Weezing the same turn to Poison it.
**Heal Block helps:** Poison, Burn, Needle, Helmet, Nightmare Aura and Hoopa's bench pings all stick.
**It hurts:** only Hoopa's Dynamite Punch recoil (nothing heals it back). Claydol on the Bench can be
Sabrina'd forward (100 HP, retreat 2). Baltoy starting Active is a weak 60 HP opener.
**Gives up:** Boiler Smog only fires when Weezing evolves from hand, so at most twice a game. A retreat or
switch wipes Poison and Burn, so a smart opponent just retreats. Eight Basics for three Bench spots: the Bench
is crowded and Claydol costs a spot you'd want for a second attacker.
**Clear flaw:** damage over time is slow against Mega ex decks that hit for 100+ and take 2 points per KO.
**Strong vs:** Espeon / Mega Gardevoir ex / Mega Altaria lines (Espeon ex's Psychic Healing is shut off; Espeon
and Gardevoir are weak to Darkness; Altaria/Diancie are Psychic and slow to start). Jolteon ex heals nothing
but its Electromagnetic Wall hurts us (see below). Mega Diancie ex / Carbink: chip sticks.
**Weak vs:** Mega Absol ex (discards our Supporter, 170 HP), Mega Sableye ex (Cursed Jewel hits back for 40),
Mega Rayquaza ex (fast, 180 HP, no weakness), Jolteon ex (its Wall does 20 each time we attach Darkness
Energy), Zoroark ex / Darkrai ex mirror (Darkness vs Darkness, no weakness help). Weezing and Koffing are weak
to Fighting, so any Fighting attacker is a problem. Mega Garchomp ex is a new promo that isn't in our card
data yet, so it isn't rated here.
**Flags:** no promo-only cards. Engine support: all cards found (Boiler Smog, Nightmare Aura, More Poison,
Heal Block, Deceptive Needle, Rocky Helmet all have code).

## Deck 2: Kingdra Lock (Water)  `list2_kingdra_lock`
2 Horsea (A4 041), 2 Seadra (A1 071), 2 Kingdra ex, 2 Baltoy, 2 Claydol, 2 Cramorant (B2 045),
2 Cyrus, 2 Misty, 2 Professor's Research, 1 Poke Ball, 1 Rare Candy

**Which Water card carries it:** Kingdra ex. Alolan Ninetales doesn't fit with Kingdra (a second Stage 1
line plus Claydol's line would crowd out the Cyrus and snipe cards), so Ninetales is Deck 3 instead.
**Plan.** Turn 1-2: Horsea (or Cramorant) up front. Cramorant's 1-Energy Spit-Out Shot puts 30 on a Benched
Pokemon, Seadra's Water Arrow puts 50 on any Pokemon. Misty speeds up the Water Energy. Turn 2-3: Baltoy then
Claydol; Horsea to Kingdra ex by Seadra or Rare Candy. Then: Cyrus pulls the damaged Mega ex Active, Kingdra
ex hits Stormy Prison (90) so it can't retreat next turn, and Claydol means it can't heal. Next turn, Prison
again, with Cramorant/Seadra finishing damage elsewhere.
**Heal Block helps:** the target can't heal with Lucky Ice Pop, Potion, Lady or Espeon ex, and can't walk out
of the Active spot. **It hurts:** we also can't heal Kingdra ex (no heals in the deck, so mild); Claydol on the
Bench is a Sabrina target; Baltoy as an opener is 60 HP.
**How often does the full line-up happen?** Rough card-count math (not a simulation): by your 4th turn you have
seen about 9 cards. The chance that by then you hold Horsea + Kingdra ex + (Seadra or Rare Candy) is about 40%.
Add Baltoy + Claydol and it is about 15%. Add Cyrus too and it is about 9%. By turn 6 (11 cards) about 25%,
by turn 8 about 50%. That is only the cards; you also need two Water Energy on Kingdra and a damaged Benched
target the opponent hasn't already moved. Realistically it comes together in perhaps 1 game in 5, and later
than the opponent's Mega ex wants to wait.
**When it doesn't line up:** Cramorant and Horsea (Hook 30) trade hits, Seadra snipes the Bench, and the deck
is just a slow Water chip deck with a healing denial. Cyrus is a dead card without a damaged Bench target.
**Strong vs:** decks that heal (Espeon ex; any Lucky Ice Pop loop), Entei (Fire is weak to Water, so Stormy Prison's 90
becomes 110 on a 110-140 HP Entei), Zoroark/Absol/Darkrai ex (Bench-heavy decks,
Cramorant pings them). **Weak vs:** Jolteon ex (Kingdra and every Water card here is weak to Lightning, and its
Wall pings our Energy attaches), Mega Rayquaza ex (fast: it can KO Kingdra's 170 HP before the plan lands), Mega Altaria/Gardevoir (big HP, Prison 90 doesn't kill).
**Flags:** no promo-only cards. Engine support: all found (Stormy Prison "can't retreat", Water Arrow snipe,
Spit-Out Shot, Cyrus, Heal Block have code).

## Deck 3: Ninetales Chip (Water)  `list3_ninetales_chip`
2 Alolan Vulpix (A3 040), 2 Alolan Ninetales (A3 041), 1 Alolan Ninetales ex, 2 Baltoy, 2 Claydol,
2 Cramorant (B2 045), 2 Cyrus, 1 Misty, 2 Professor's Research, 2 Poke Ball, 1 Rocky Helmet, 1 Giovanni

**Plan.** Spread damage and let it stay. Turn 1: Vulpix; going second, attach Water and use Call Forth Cold
(a second Water Energy on itself). Turn 2: evolve to Alolan Ninetales, attach the third, Blizzard for 60 and 20
to EACH Benched Pokemon. Claydol arrives turn 2-3. With a full opposing Bench that is 60 + 60 a turn, and no
Lucky Ice Pop can take it back. Alolan Ninetales ex's Binding Snow (80, WW) stops them attaching Energy to the
Active, which is the best answer to Mega ex decks that need Energy each turn. Cyrus drags a damaged Bench
Pokemon up so the spread turns into Knock Outs. Cramorant adds 30 to a Bench spot for just one Energy.
**Heal Block helps:** all Bench damage is permanent, opponents' heals are dead cards, Rocky Helmet chips
stay. **It hurts:** no heals of ours, so nothing lost, but Ninetales/Claydol on the Bench get Sabrina'd.
**Gives up:** 3-Energy Blizzard is slow if Vulpix gets hit before it evolves; there are only 2 Vulpix for 3
Ninetales cards; Ninetales is weak to Metal; Baltoy/Claydol take up to 4 deck slots.
**Strong vs:** Bench-heavy decks (Mega Altaria/Espeon/Darkrai, Zoroark/Absol/Darkrai, Mega Gardevoir/Diancie/
Carbink) because Blizzard hits all of them; Binding Snow slows Mega Rayquaza, Gardevoir and Diancie. Entei
(Fire is weak to Water). **Weak vs:** Jolteon ex (its Wall does 20 to each Pokémon we attach Energy to, and Blizzard needs 3; only
Cramorant is weak to Lightning, since Ninetales is weak to Metal),
Mega Rayquaza ex (just races us), Mega Sableye/Absol (hit-and-recoil plus Supporter discard), any deck
whose Bench is empty or small (the spread does nothing).
**Flags:** no promo-only cards. Engine support: all found (Blizzard bench hit, Binding Snow, Call Forth Cold,
Heal Block). I judge this the most realistic of the three: it needs fewer pieces in sequence.
