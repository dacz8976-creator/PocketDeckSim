## 1. What this answers

The plan (`docs/REVIEW_2026-09-24_direction.md`, section 8, the Skarmory Tool and Jasmine bullet, line 130) proposes a reserve adoption route for a bot change the 28-cell table can barely see. Its clause (d), verbatim:

> (d) A gain beyond paired noise on the new bot's own side, and, by the second Fable session's change accepted by Fable, **on at least one Limitless top-30 archetype that is not Dustin's deck and carries the relevant cards**, as a requirement and not a list entry; deck 07 and Dustin's other decks are reported too, but since the causal test already showed the Skarmory gain (+5.7, higher in all eight matchups) a gain on his own decks is the motivation for the fix rather than a test of it, and a route judged only on his decks would over several fixes build a pilot specifically good at his decks and a screen that passes his brews too readily. Where that archetype has real Limitless cells against the eight table decks, those cells are reported before and after, a reported figure and not a gate, since the table can see the fix through them even though the eight lists cannot.

The closure sentence, fixed before this census was run, verbatim from the same bullet:

> Stated up front, before any census is read: if the card census finds no Limitless top-30 archetype outside Dustin's decks that carries reduction Tools or turn-effect cards, or finds one with no usable decklist, the route is closed for that candidate and adoption can come only by Dustin's explicit override, recorded as such, as with kp3; the route is not loosened after the census is seen.

The candidate is kt (`rl/results/fable_reviews_2026-09-26/kt_spec_review.md`). Its switch 1 (diagnostic code `kta`) prices the defender's temporary damage cuts in the threat clock; switch 2 (`ktb`) replaces the flat +10 for a Tool on the Active; switch 3 (`ktc`) prices damage back to the attacker. The kt review's own copy of the route (lines 198 to 207) applies the closure sentence to switch 1: "if the carrier census finds no such archetype, or one with no usable decklist, the route is closed for switch 1 and it is adoptable only by Dustin's explicit override, recorded as such; the route is not loosened after the census is seen."

One wording point, reported and not resolved here: the section 8 closure sentence names "reduction Tools or turn-effect cards" (groups 2 and 1 below), while clause (d) and the kt review say "the relevant cards", and the kt draft's switch 1 prices a third kind through the same hook, the Pokémon whose own attack cuts damage to itself for the opponent's next turn (group 3). The census counted all three groups. The two carriers that matter most below (Gouging Fire, Frigibax) are group 3; the only group-1 carrier outside Dustin's decks is Blue in four Vespiquen lists. Whether group 3 counts as "the relevant cards" under the closure sentence is a reading of fixed rule text, and that reading is Dustin's.

Three of the 35 archetypes carry a switch-1 card in at least one list. Every other archetype, Dustin's or not, has 0.

**Dragonair Mega Rayquaza ex: a natural carrier.** 135 of its 143 development lists (94%) carry Gouging Fire B3a 054 (132 with 2 copies, 3 with 1; 267 copies), in 41 of its 44 events. Six more lists carry only Poncho or Rocky Helmet (group 4), so 141 of 143 carry something from the set. Sept 10 rank 22 with 56 players; window rank 6 by development matches (818 decisive-or-tie; 857 every-row). A usable list exists (section 6). It is marked Dustin's in `census.csv` by one rule only:

- **Reading 1, the census's conservative rule (`shares_pokemon`).** Dragonair is in the archetype name and 2 Dragonair B4 117 are in `decks/dustin/11-archaludon-haxorus-dragonair.txt`, so `census.csv` marks it `dustin=True`; the recount's hand-written map gives the same verdict and labels it CONSERVATIVE ("Dragonair B4 117 is a named main of decks/dustin/11; no Mega Rayquaza ex in his files").
