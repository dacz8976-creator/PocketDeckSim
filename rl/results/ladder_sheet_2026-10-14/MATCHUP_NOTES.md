# Matchup notes for the ladder (Oct 14)

Eight short notes taken from your own pause games. Each is one real turn of yours where the bots would have played differently from you.
For each: what the situation was, what you did, what the bots did, which is better and why, and the rule of thumb it suggests.

**Read this first.**
- **None of these turns was played with brew 07, deck 12, deck 13 or brew 10.** They are from your older decks (Kingdra ex / Weezing ex, Aegislash,
  Conkeldurr, and others). Each note says honestly which cards, or which kind of situation, carries over to your ladder decks.
- **The bots are hints, not judges.** "The bots play X" never decides a note by itself. What decides it is the card text and the plain
  consequence: a sure knock-out, an Energy that gets lost, a hit that ends the game. In notes 2 and 4, and in half of note 3, the bots are the ones who are wrong.
- **How sure each note is:** *clear* = the card text and the board settle it; *I think* = settled on the board, but there is a reason on the other side;
  *judgment call* = both lines are defensible, and the note says what each risks. Where the bots' line is better, the note says so, and none of it is blame.
  Where you were right, the note says why.
- **"The strong bot"** is kx3 and **"the everyday bot"** is km3, as on the ladder sheet. **"The newer test version"** is the candidate version of the strong bot.
- **Points:** a Mega ex knock-out is worth 3 points, an ordinary ex 2, anything else 1. That matters in several notes.

## 1. Swap in your loaded attacker when it gives an exact knock-out
*(Aegislash / Melmetal ex game, your 5th turn)*

- **Situation:** 0-0. Their Haxorus in front has 80 HP left, and it is about to get a Fighting Energy (then it hits for 150). Your Magneton (2 Energy)
  is in front. Your Melmetal ex (2 Energy) waits on the Bench. You hold the turn's Energy.
- **You:** Energy onto Melmetal ex on the Bench, Magneton pokes for 30. Haxorus is left on 50.
- **The bots:** all three, every single time: retreat Magneton, bring up Melmetal ex, give it the Energy, Headbutt for exactly 80. A sure knock-out.
- **Better?** The bots, and I'd call it clear. Your reason is a fair one: keep the ex safe on the Bench while a cheaper Pokémon chips. But that works only
  while the chipper survives, and with their Fighting Energy coming it would not. Magneton and its two Energy died to Haxorus's next hit (150), which also gave
  them their first point, so the retreat cost you nothing extra. After the bots' line, their second Haxorus could do about 120 to 140 to Melmetal ex, leaving it
  on 30 to 50 of its 170 HP. The only worry is a card in their hand that adds damage.
- **Rule of thumb:** before you chip with the Pokémon in front, check whether swapping in your loaded Pokémon gives the knock-out. If it does, pay the retreat.
- **For your decks:** no shared cards, the situation carries. Hoopa ex, Darkrai ex, Alolan Ninetales ex and Giratina ex retreat for 2 Energy. Mega Sableye ex,
  Whimsicott ex, Teal Mask Ogerpon ex and Mega Diancie ex retreat for 1. Team Rocket's Raticate ex retreats for free. A Mega ex pulled forward and
  knocked out costs you 3 points, so check that one first.

## 2. One knock-out from losing? Count what your attack costs you before you swing
*(Conkeldurr / Garganacl game, your 5th turn)*

- **Situation:** you have 0 points and they have 2, so one knock-out of any of your Pokémon loses the game. Their Mega Absol ex (110 HP left, Darkness Claw
  ready for 80, Poison Barb attached) is in front. It is worth 3 points, your whole game. You have Timburr in front, with Gurdurr and Conkeldurr in hand.
- **You:** evolve to Gurdurr, attach the Energy, end the turn with no attack.
- **The bots:** the same, then attack with Strength. All three, every time.
- **Better?** You, clearly. Poison Barb poisons any Pokémon whose attack damages its holder while the holder is in front. A poisoned Gurdurr loses 10 at the
  check-up (20 with their Nihilego on the Bench), so Claw's 80 finishes it and they win. By not attacking you kept Gurdurr at 90 HP. It dropped to 10, and next turn
  Conkeldurr's Bedrock Breaker (100, plus 20 from your Stadium) knocked out the Mega. Your 4th-turn attack was different, and it was right: they had 1 point then, not 2, and
  your Conkeldurr (70 HP left) was facing Claw's 80 either way. That attack's 100 damage is what left the Mega low enough (110 after two Ice Pops) for Bedrock Breaker to
  finish. I can't tell why all three bots attack. The everyday bot prefers it only a little, and its score for the attack shows no sign of their winning knock-out. The two
  strong bots play the game out in simulation and still attack, and I can't explain that. Either way, the card text settles this turn, not the bots.
- **Rule of thumb:** when they are one knock-out from winning, add the Poison damage to the hit you will take before you attack.
- **For your decks:** Poison Barb is not in the eight lists on the ladder sheet, but real opponents do play it (this one did), so this is a "know the trap" note.
  Field Blower (brew 07, deck 13, brew 10) removes the Barb first, so play it before you attack. Pokémon Center Lady (brew 07, brew 10) cures Poison only on your next
  turn, after the check-up has already hit, so she won't get you out of this trap. Teal Mask Ogerpon ex's Soothing Wind (deck 12) stops Poison on your Pokémon that have
  Energy, as long as Ogerpon ex is in play.
- **Rules change:** this turn is flagged for Monday's change only because a Guts Pokémon (Conkeldurr A3 096) is in your deck. The same flag is on every turn of this game.
  It is in your discard pile here, nothing in your deck brings it back, and this turn you evolve into Gurdurr. The Conkeldurr in your hand is the Bedrock Breaker one, with
  no Guts. So the change does not touch the turn.

## 3. Sabrina is a true gust only when they have exactly one Benched Pokémon
*(Kingdra ex / Weezing ex game, your 2nd turn; the "other side" is a different turn of a different game)*

- **Situation:** 0-0. Their Suicune ex (140 HP) is in front, and their only Benched Pokémon is a 60-HP Frigibax. You have a Koffing in front (a Small Balloon
  makes its retreat free), and Sabrina, Weezing ex and a Horsea in hand.
- **You:** Sabrina (Frigibax has to come up), free retreat, Weezing ex poisons and burns Frigibax, Hook (from Horsea): 30 + 10 + 20 = 60. A sure knock-out, one point.
- **The bots:** the strong bot opens with Sabrina in 10 of 12 replays and the newer test version in 11 of 12; the test stops at Sabrina, so what they do next is
  not shown. The everyday bot never plays her: it poisons and burns the big ex instead, which scores no point.
- **Better?** You (and the strong bot's opening), I think. The same damage becomes a point nobody can heal. The other line leaves the ex on about 80 HP, and the
  next turn the opponent played Soothing Shore (a Stadium that heals 20 each turn) and a Giant Cape (+20 HP) on it. One thing to know: Sabrina moving a Pokémon to
  the Bench cures its Poison, so gust first and poison second.
- **The other side (a different game):** at 1-1, their fully loaded Dragonite (160 HP) stood in front of a lone 60-HP Dratini. The everyday bot gusted the Dratini for a
  sure point every time, and the strong bot in 8 of 12; the newer test version attacked the Dragonite, like you, in 9 of 12. You hit the Dragonite and poisoned it. I
  can't call this one: it is a judgment call. Either way their Dragonite fires its four random 50-damage hits once more (in your game that killed a Horsea and left your Kingdra
  ex on 20 HP). The difference is that yours died on your next turn, while a gusted Dragonite stays at full health and fires again.
- **Rule of thumb:** with one Benched Pokémon, Sabrina is a sure gust: use it when that target is one you can knock out. Before gusting a small target, ask what their
  loaded attacker does next.
- **For your decks:** deck 13 has Sabrina (one copy) and Cyrus. Brew 07 has two Cyrus and a Repel, brew 10 one Cyrus. Cyrus is a true gust, but only onto a damaged
  Benched Pokémon. Repel, like Sabrina, lets them choose. Once charged, Binding Snow (80) and Boost Dash (70) knock out any Basic up to 70 HP in one hit. Poison and Burn are
  not in your decks, so only the gust idea carries.

## 4. Copycat is for a hand you are done with
*(Scolipede / Mega Sableye ex game, your 1st turn, with your 2nd, 3rd and 4th for contrast)*

- **Situation:** turn 1, going first. Your Venipede is alone in front. You hold Copycat, Wally, Whirlipede and two Scolipede. They hold 4 cards.
- **You:** end the turn and keep the Copycat.
- **The bots:** play Copycat in 11 of 12 replays (the newer version in 12 of 12). The everyday bot barely prefers it (a near-tie). It repeats on your 2nd and 3rd turns:
  the bots reach for Copycat each time, and you held it each time.
- **Better?** You, I think. Copycat shuffles your hand into the deck and gives back as many cards as THEY hold: four. This hand already was your plan. After a
  Copycat you would have the Whirlipede in time for turn 3 only about half the time. The Whirlipede by turn 3 and the Scolipede by turn 5 together would come about
  one time in four. With Wally as well, about one in fifteen. On your 4th turn you did play Copycat, with a dead Scolipede and Cyrus in hand, which fits the same rule.
- **Rule of thumb:** Copycat is for a hand you are done with, not one that holds next turn's evolution. When you do play it, play your Items first, and make the Energy
  and evolution choices after you have seen the new cards (note 6).
- **For your decks:** Copycat is in all four. Deck 12 and deck 13 (a Basic plus its evolution in hand) are closest. Brew 07 and brew 10 have only Basics, so there
  the same rule means: don't shuffle away the ex you want to bench next turn, or a Supporter you already know you'll use.
- **Rules change:** this game is flagged for Monday's change only because of Mega Sableye ex's Cursed Jewel. No Sableye attacks in the Copycat decisions, so the
  change does not touch them.

## 5. Add the +20 Weakness before you decide you can't knock out
*(Mega Blaziken list game, your 2nd turn)*

- **Situation:** 0-0. Your Torchic (40 HP left, Rocky Helmet) is in front and Castform is on the Bench. Their Tinkatink in front has 40 HP left, 1 Energy, and is weak to Fire.
  You hold Combusken, Flame Patch and two Research.
- **You:** evolve Torchic into Combusken, Research, bench a Torchic, Fire on Combusken. No attack (Combusken needs two Fire).
- **The bots:** the test's plans stop at the draw card (all three play Research first, 12 of 12). With the draw cards taken out, the everyday bot and the newer test version
  took a knock-out every time we ran them and the strong bot in 5 of 6 runs. They get it by retreating Torchic, moving the Fire to Castform with Flame Patch, and
  evolving the benched Torchic, all in one turn (30 + 20).
- **Better?** The knock-out, and it is clear that it was there. Fire on Torchic, Peck 20 + 20 Weakness = exactly 40. You took the same point on your next turn with the
  Castform trick and won (they gave up), so it cost you a turn and some HP on Combusken. That is a trade of time, not a mistake. You were building toward Mega Blaziken, and
  you used the same +20 the next turn.
- **Rule of thumb:** add the +20 before you say "I can't knock it out". Weakness counts on their Active Pokémon only.
- **For your decks:** none of these Pokémon are in your decks; the habit carries. Alolan Vulpix and Alolan Ninetales ex (deck 13) are Water, so they get +20 on every
  Pokémon in the Blaziken list: Gnaw goes from 20 to 40 and Binding Snow from 80 to 100. (Rattata and Raticate ex are Colorless and get nothing.) The bots find Blaziken the
  hardest list for deck 12 (40% and 18% on the sheet), and Grass is weak to Fire. Rocky Helmet's damage does not get Weakness.

## 6. Draw first, commit last
*(Kingdra ex / Weezing ex game, your 3rd turn)*

- **Situation:** 0-0. Your Seadra (1 Energy) leads and a Horsea is behind it. Their Pichu (30 HP) is in front, with a Dragonair (80 HP, 3 Energy) on their Bench and 5 cards
  in their hand. You hold Poké Ball, Copycat, Sabrina, Rare Candy and two Weezing ex.
- **You:** Poké Ball, Copycat, evolve Seadra into Kingdra ex, Energy, Stormy Prison. Pichu is down.
- **The bots:** the everyday bot retreats Seadra first every time (12 of 12), the strong bot 10 of 12, and the newer test version only 5 of 12 (it often opens with Copycat,
  like you). The retreat leads to Energy on Horsea and Hook for exactly 30, the same point. The everyday bot's scores show it sees that knock-out, though its plan stops
  at the Poké Ball.
- **Better?** You, I think. Small Balloon only helps Basic Pokémon, so that retreat really cost the Seadra its only Energy. Drawing first would find Kingdra ex about 7 times in
  10 (it did). If it had not, the retreat-and-Hook plan was still there afterwards. The price: Copycat shuffled away two Weezing ex, Sabrina and Rare Candy.
  That is why it is "I think": here you needed the Kingdra ex now, which is the opposite of note 4's hand.
- **Rule of thumb:** play your draw cards first, then decide the retreat, the Energy and the evolution. Poké Ball before Copycat, because Copycat shuffles your hand away.
  It works when what you hope to draw is worth more than the hand you shuffle away. Skip it when you need the Supporter slot for something else or your Bench is full.
- **For your decks:** Poké Ball, Professor's Research and Copycat are in all four.

## 7. Before you evolve your attacker, read the new attack's cost
*(Kingdra ex / Weezing ex game, your 2nd turn)*

- **Situation:** 0-0. Their Nihilego has 40 HP left of 70. Your Horsea (1 Energy) is in front, and Weezing ex and Seadra are in your hand. Their Magmar waits on the Bench.
- **You:** Horsea into Seadra, Koffing into Weezing ex (Poison and Burn on Nihilego). No attack: this Seadra needs 3 Energy.
- **The bots:** the everyday bot, every time: Weezing ex, evolve the Horsea on the Bench instead, keep the front Horsea, and use Hook. The strong bot ends with Hook in 10 of 12
  replays and the newer test version in 11 of 12.
- **Better?** The bots' line, I think. Hook 30 leaves Nihilego on 10, and Poison and Burn take 30 between turns: a sure knock-out. Yours got no point that turn (in the game they
  moved Nihilego away on their turn). It readied Kingdra ex a turn sooner in general, but you held Rare Candy too, so here the gap was small. How much that speed is worth is the
  judgment call, since the point is sure.
- **Rule of thumb:** before you evolve the Pokémon that is about to attack, read the new card's attack cost. If you can't pay it this turn, evolve a Bench copy and attack
  first. Add end-of-turn damage (Poison, Burn, Deceptive Needle) to your attack.
- **For your decks:** Cottonee's Razor Leaf costs one Grass, Whimsicott ex's Grass Knot two. Alolan Vulpix's Gnaw costs one Water, Ninetales ex's Binding Snow two. Rattata's
  Ambush costs one, Raticate ex's Boost Dash two. So if your Energy for the turn is already used, or you are going first on turn 1, evolving a Pokémon that has only one
  Energy can cost you the attack. Spinarak's Sting and Ariados's Pierce both cost one Grass, so that evolution never costs you the attack. In brew 07, Hoopa ex's
  Shadow Bullet (30) plus a Deceptive Needle (10) is 40.

## 8. When one knock-out ends the game, count their biggest hit plus a Supporter that adds damage
*(Snorlax ex / Terapagos ex game, your 5th turn)*

- **Situation:** you have 0 points and they have 2, so any knock-out ends it. Your Terapagos ex (140 of 150 HP) is in front of a Happiny (30 HP). Your Bench has Snorlax ex (160 HP,
  no Energy) and Silvally. Their loaded Garchomp waits on their Bench, and Happiny's +20 is active, so Garchomp's Land Crush hits for 120 + 20 = 140. You hold Lucky Ice Pop,
  Persian and Type: Null.
- **You:** Rainbow Cave (Metal again), Lucky Ice Pop (140 to 150; heads, so it came back to your hand), retreat to Snorlax ex (paying the Grass and the Metal),
  Metal on Snorlax ex, Type: Null to the Bench, no attack.
- **The bots:** all three, every time: Type: Null, Lucky Ice Pop, Energy on Terapagos, Prism Impact for 120. Happiny is gone, a free point.
- **Better?** A judgment call. Healed to 150, Terapagos survives Garchomp's 140 with 10 HP, and so does Snorlax ex (160). Giovanni's +10 knocks out Terapagos (150) but not
  Snorlax ex, which is the one case where the retreat wins. Red's +20 or Cynthia's +50 (they played Cynthia on their next turn, which you could not see) beats both. Retreating
  also cost you the sure point and two Energy. The bots' agreement is no proof: the everyday bot only estimates their hit, and the strong bot guesses their hand from eight fixed
  lists that don't even have Garchomp, let alone those Supporters.
- **Rule of thumb:** when one knock-out ends the game, count their biggest hit plus any Supporter that adds damage. Lucky Ice Pop heals only the Pokémon in front, so decide who
  stays in front first.
- **For your decks:** Lucky Ice Pop is in brew 07 (two) and brew 10 (one). It heals 20 from the Pokémon in front, only if that one is hurt, and on heads it comes back to your
  hand. Darkrai ex has 140 HP (160 while your Starting Plains is in play; their own Stadium replaces it), so without the Plains a 140 hit knocks it out even at full health.
  A Mega Sableye ex (170 HP, 190 under Starting Plains) or Mega Diancie ex (170 HP; brew 10's Giant Cape and Starting Plains add 20 each, up to 210) knocked out is 3 points,
  so the "one knock-out ends it" counting starts earlier.
- **Rules change:** this turn is flagged for Monday's change only because your list has a Meowth with Carefree Steps. Its only copy was knocked out on their 3rd turn (its coin
  came up tails) and is in your discard pile, so it can't come up this turn or in their reply.
