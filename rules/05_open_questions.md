# 05 — Open questions, conflicts, and how to settle them

Everything here is either unanswered by any source I could reach, or answered differently by different sources.
Ordered by how much it could change simulated games. "Test" = a check Dustin (or anyone) could do in the app,
usually in a Solo battle or a friend battle; the attack preview ("Expected damage") shows damage without attacking.
The tests are collected with setups in `08_tests_to_record.md`.

Historical note from the 2026-09-22 second session: #6 was answered by community statistics; `unified1` had incorrect
Checkup KO timing. Rules1 repaired that behavior and active rules4 retains the fix. Community evidence was added to #2;
questions #19–#28 came from the full engine audit (`07_engine_audit_2026-09-22.md`).

## High impact

**2. Simultaneous win conditions — T2 narrow case settled 2026-09-22; broader counting model remains unverified.**
- Official: points win only if reached "before the other player"; a player with no Pokémon left loses "regardless of the number of points each player has". Result table in `01_game_structure.md` §6.
- **Observed T2 case [OBSERVED, one video seat]:** Dustin had 2 points and one Pokémon left; the attack awarded his 3rd point and Knocked Out that Pokémon in the same exchange. The opponent still had Pokémon, and the result overlay showed Tie. The opponent's final 2 points are inferred from the ex Knock Out, not read on the overlay. Both-seat engine fixtures are regression coverage, not video observations. Evidence: `../Boss Folder/rules4-t2-repair-2026-09-22/T2-evidence.md`.
- The three readings before the test (kept for the record):
  - In-app Tips, literal: the player with no Pokémon left loses → **the defender wins**.
  - Japanese guides (torecataru.com; a Game8 JP summary) [COMMUNITY]: "相打ちで片方が3ポイントに到達し、もう一方が到達していない場合、3ポイントのプレイヤーが勝利" (in a mutual KO, if only one player reaches 3, that player wins) → **the attacker wins**. This matches Dustin's expectation. Neither source says it covers the attacker's *last* Pokémon.
  - pokemon-zone's "count the win conditions" model [COMMUNITY] also predicts the observed T2 result: the attacker meets the 3-point condition and the defender meets the "opponent has no Pokémon" condition. This one matching case is consistent with the model but does not validate its broader variants.
  - What 225430 shows: the Knocked-Out Pokémon stays at 0 HP while Rocky Helmet and Destiny Burst resolve, both leave play together, then points are given turn player first. If the game checks for a win as each point is given, the attacker's 3rd point ends it first.
- Both reach 3 at the same moment with Pokémon left → **tie**. Now [COMMUNITY] from several independent sources (poke-memo.com, Game8 JP, GameWith, Dexerto citing a real Reddit game), none contradicting. Dustin (2026-09-25, `rl/results/recordings_check_2026-09-25/OWNER_ANSWERS.md` on main): both reaching 3 on one attack is a Tie, citing his video (the T2 recording, about 20 tries). Engine: Tie. Matches.
- Both run out of Pokémon at once → tie (Dexerto; poke-memo: "UNLESS one player has no Pokémon remaining, in which case that player loses instead").
- Not affected: Poison/Burn only hit at Checkup, after the turn, so a player who already took the 3rd point has won by then.
- **Rules4 implementation:** when exactly one player reaches at least 3 points, that same player has no Pokémon, and the opponent still has Pokémon and fewer than 3 points, the result is a tie. See the [rules4 repair record](../Boss%20Folder/rules4-t2-repair-2026-09-22/README.md). Rules4 does not establish both-empty or both-at-least-3 variants; those broader community/inferred cases remain unverified.

**4. Order inside Pokémon Checkup between Special Conditions and "During Pokémon Checkup" Abilities** (Flygon ex Sand Slammer, Garganacl Blessed Salt, Glaceon ex Snowy Terrain). — **Poison before the heal: SETTLED 2026-09-22 [OBSERVED 010316].**
- Settled [OFFICIAL, in-app Tips]: Checkup checks both players' Pokémon, applies Checkup Abilities/Trainers, the player whose turn just ended checks Special Conditions first, and **Knock Outs happen only at the end of the Checkup**. End-of-turn effects come before the Checkup [OBSERVED].
- **Settled [OBSERVED 010316]: Poison resolves first, then Blessed Salt.** Dustin's Poisoned Passimian ex had two Garganacl on his Bench. At T16 it started at 130 HP and the Checkup went "Poison 130→120, then two Blessed Salt heals return it to 130". At T18 it started at 120 HP and went "Poison 120→110, then two Blessed Salt heals to 130". These observations establish Poison-before-Blessed-Salt in this scenario; they do not establish Burn's order with healing. Active rules4 uses Poison/Burn → Snowy Terrain → Blessed Salt → one Knock Out wave. Still unobserved: a lethal status-damage case saved by healing under the official end-of-Checkup KO rule, and whose Checkup Abilities go first when both players have one (only Dustin had one here).
- The Poison-before-Blessed-Salt order is settled by 010316. Still unresolved: the order between the two players' Checkup Abilities when both players have one, and whether other Checkup Abilities such as Snowy Terrain resolve before or after Special Conditions. The Tips page lists them in one sentence without an order. The web search (2026-09-22, ~15 queries in EN/JP naming each card) found nothing Pocket-specific; the only order found was the physical TCG's (conditions → Abilities → KOs), which doesn't count here.
- Historical engine finding: `unified1` Knocked a Pokémon Out as soon as Poison/Burn took it to 0, before Blessed Salt (`07` M1). This was repaired in rules1 and remains fixed in active rules4, which defers Checkup KOs until all Checkup effects finish. A Poisoned Pokémon with 10 HP and Garganacl should therefore survive the Checkup under either damage/heal order.
- Optional confirmation (`08` T3): test a Poisoned Pokémon on exactly 10 HP with Garganacl in play. Survival at 10 HP is expected from the official end-of-Checkup KO rule and confirms lethal-boundary behavior in the app; it does not re-test the now-observed Poison-before-Blessed-Salt order. Burn-before-heal remains inferred, not observed.

**21. Who promotes first when both Actives are Knocked Out during Pokémon Checkup.** (New.)
- One Japanese guide (gamepedia.jp, "ポケモンチェックとは？同時にきぜつした場合…") [SINGLE]: "次に番が来るプレイヤーが最初にバトル場に出すポケモンを選び、その後ターンを終えたばかりのプレイヤーが…" — the player whose turn is **next** chooses first, then the player who just finished.
- Compare: after a double KO *during an attack*, the attacker (the player whose turn it still is) promotes first [DUSTIN + OBSERVED 225430]. That doesn't contradict the guide, because a Checkup belongs to neither player's turn, but the only source for the Checkup case is one guide page with no test described. Treat it as unconfirmed.
- Engine: attack double KOs are attacker-first since rules1; Checkup double KOs are still in seat order, marked open in the code. Test: `08` T4.

## Medium impact

**6. Opening hand: how the other four cards are drawn — SETTLED 2026-09-22 [COMMUNITY-TESTED + OBSERVED T1]; the `unified1` engine does it wrong.**
- Settled before: at least one Basic is guaranteed, every time (Dustin: a deck with a single Basic always opens with it).
- New: two independent statistical studies on real games. machapin (Qiita, 2,000 automated games, v1.2.5) tested (1) "one Basic first, then 4 random", (2) "redeal until a Basic appears", (3) "deal 5; if no Basic, swap one card for a Basic". Model 3 fits both test decks (p = 0.18 and 0.91). Models 1 and 2 are rejected; Model 1 at p ≈ 10⁻³⁰ (2-Basic deck: both Basics 6.2% observed vs 21.1% under Model 1). Davoi (Qiita, 120 games) saw 8%, also ruling out Model 1.
- The engine uses Model 1 (`deck.rs` `shuffle(initial_shuffle=true)`), as did Dustin's guess. For a 4-Basic deck it opens with 2+ Basics 53% of the time vs ~25% for real. Full table in `07` H1.
- **Confirmed on the current app, 2026-09-22 (Dustin's test T1):** 2-Basic deck (2 Riolu + 18 Trainers, verified from its QR code), 50 opening hands, both Riolu in **3** (6%). Basic-first predicts ~10.5 (P(≤3) = 0.35%, rejected); swap-in predicts 2.6 (fits). Counts in `_research_notes/audit_2026-09-22/T1_T10_results.md`. **Settled [OBSERVED + COMMUNITY-TESTED].**

**9. Hand full when an effect returns a card to hand** ("Hand full; unable to return card"). Where does the card go (stays in play? discarded?) Playing a card frees a slot first, so Lucky Ice Pop, Koga or Ilima returning one card can't overflow a hand on their own. Likely trigger [INFERRED]: returning an **evolved** Pokémon (2+ cards) — e.g. hold 10, play Ilima (9 left), return an evolved Colorless Pokémon (+2 = 11). Watch where the extra card goes (`08` T5). The 2026-09-22 web search (EN/JP) found no discussion of this case. Settled part: **drawing** at 10 cards — the draw is skipped and the card stays in the deck (in-game text "Hand full; unable to draw cards"; Dustin agrees; engine ✓). Professor's Research at 10 cards: Game8 JP says draws past 10 are simply skipped and "「博士の研究」などを使っても11枚以上持つことはできない", which implies it can be played and draws what fits [COMMUNITY]; the engine allows it and caps the draw ✓.

**11. Weakness when attacker-side debuffs take the damage to 0** ("Attacks do −20 damage" on a 20-damage attack). Settled part: an attack whose own damage is 0 (e.g. "X damage for each Heads" with all Tails) does 0 — no +20 Weakness [DUSTIN + COMMUNITY GameWith]; engine ✓ (`hooks/core.rs` returns 0 before any modifier). Still open: an attack that does damage but is debuffed to 0 by an attacker-side effect. The engine gives 20 (0 + Weakness). No new source 2026-09-22. Test: damage preview (`08` T7).

**12. Order within step 4** when a Pokémon has both a flat reduction and a threshold prevention. The only threshold cards are Silcoon and Cascoon (B1): their attack says "During your opponent's next turn, prevent all damage done to this Pokémon by attacks if that damage is 40 or less." Question: if that Pokémon also has a "takes −20 damage from attacks" effect and is hit for 60, does it take 0 (−20 first → 40 → prevented) or 40 (threshold checked first → 60 not prevented → −20)? Weakness is added before either (official order), so a 30-damage attack that hits Weakness is 50 and isn't prevented. Very rare — Silcoon/Cascoon are seldom played.

**19. Heavy Helmet and a changed Retreat Cost — SETTLED 2026-09-25 [OBSERVED, Dustin's test `Battle Logs/heavyhelmet_test.MP4`: printed Retreat Cost 3, Peculiar Plaza in play, a 40-damage attack did 40].** Text: "If the Pokémon this card is attached to has a Retreat Cost of 3 or more…". It uses the **current** Retreat Cost, like attacks that depend on it: no −20 when Peculiar Plaza takes it below 3 (seen), and by the same rule −20 when Ariados (or Goo-zooka) raises it to 3 (Dustin's answer, not separately recorded). The engine reads the **printed** cost (`hooks/core.rs`, `heavy_helmet_reduction`), an open bug on `09`, to fix after kpr's table is read. Observed before: it switched on/off as evolution changed the printed cost (first-altaria). Exposure: Dustin's deck 12 (Ariados), and any Helmet holder under Plaza (brews 05b, 10). Test: `08` T8. The separate `unified1` non-attack-damage defect (`07` H2) was repaired in rules1 and remains fixed in active rules4.

**23. Who picks the Energy for untyped discards — RULE SETTLED; IMPLEMENTED IN RULES3.** [DUSTIN + OBSERVED, T6 video `EnergyRetreatAttackChoice`, reviewed 2026-09-22]. The video shows four choices, each prompted after the damage or cost: Gouging Fire's Scorching Interruption on Fire/Fire/Lightning ("Please choose 2 Energy"; Fire + Lightning picked), Walking Wake's Sweeping Billow on Fire/Water (the player highlighted Fire, switched to Water, then confirmed), and retreat both with Small Balloon (1 Energy) and without (2). For retreat and an attack that says "discard X Energy" without "random", the player chooses which attached Energy to discard. Active rules4 implements these choices. All-Energy-of-one-type cases were not separately shown, but offer no distinct physical Energy choice. See `09` and the [rules3 repair record](../Boss%20Folder/rules3-repairs-2026-09-22/README.md).

**29. Hiking Trail and Legendary Pulse at the end of the same turn — CONFLICT, pending Dustin's check (2026-09-25).** The Suicune ex player ends its turn with Hiking Trail in play ("At the end of each player's turn, that player draws cards until they have 3 cards in their hand") and 0–2 cards in hand, Suicune ex Active (Legendary Pulse: "At the end of your turn, if this Pokémon is in the Active Spot, draw a card"). Dustin's gut: Pulse first, then Hiking Trail tops up last, ending on **3** cards. The engine: Hiking Trail draws at once and Pulse's draw is queued, resolving after the turn change, ending on **4** (`hooks/core.rs`, the end-of-turn hooks). **Table impact:** the Suicune v Blaziken cell (Blaziken's list runs Hiking Trail), and Dustin's Hiking Trail decks 06, 10 and 12. Test: one look, in a friend battle with both in play.

**22. Turn-limit timing.** (New.) Settled: 30 turns counting both players' turns → tie [OFFICIAL tie; COMMUNITY number; an X post quoting the in-app rules text: "Games … can last a maximum of 30 turns. If you reach the limit, the duel ends in a tie."]. Solo is 50 per Game8 JP, disputed by one Yahoo answer ("30"). Unknown: does the 30th turn's end-of-turn step and Checkup still happen, and can a KO there still win? Engine: it runs the final Checkup, and a Checkup win stands; the tie applies only when turn 31 would begin (`state/mod.rs` `advance_turn`, `if self.turn_count > 30`). Rare in practice. Dustin (2026-09-25): "I would think it would be a tie, but almost never happens in real life"; recorded against the engine and dropped as too rare to matter.

## Low impact / settled enough

- **24. Random searches: uniform over cards or over names?** (New.) Poké Ball "a random Basic Pokémon from your deck": with 2 Riolu + 1 Hitmonlee, is Riolu 2/3 or 1/2? No source. The engine gives each card an equal chance (2/3), the natural reading of "random card". Would take ~100 logged Poké Balls to test; not worth it.
- **25. "Once during your turn" after a Pokémon leaves play and comes back the same turn** (e.g. returned by Ilima and replayed). (New.) No Pocket source. A different copy of the same card can use its own Ability [SINGLE, Yahoo answer, may be physical TCG]. The engine treats a replayed Pokémon as new (can use it again). Rarely possible.
- **26. Which discard pile a replaced Stadium goes to.** Its owner's [INFERRED, physical-TCG rule]; no Pocket source (searched 2026-09-22). Matters only for discard-pile counting effects.
- **27. Bidoof's Super Fang "Halve … remaining HP, rounded down".** The engine rounds down to a multiple of 10 (70 → 30). HP and damage are always multiples of 10 in Pocket, so this is probably right; unconfirmed.
- **28. Heal targets — SETTLED [DUSTIN, stated before and again 2026-09-22]:** no healing card can be used on a full-HP Pokémon; only Pokémon Center Lady may target a full-HP Pokémon, and only if it has a Special Condition. `09` lists the Potion/Erika/Lillie/Marlon target fix as done. Original question: The engine lets Potion/Erika/Lillie/Marlon choose it; Pokémon Center Lady/Ilima can't. Only wastes a card. One look: `08` T9.
- **20. "Moved from the Bench to the Active Spot this turn" after promoting during your *own* turn.** (New.) The official Scizor ruling covers promotion on the opponent's turn (doesn't count) and moving up then evolving (doesn't count). Engine gets both right. Unknown: your own Active is Knocked Out during your turn (recoil, Ability self-damage, Fossil discarded), you promote, then attack with Scizor/Golisopod/Crobat/Flutter Mane. Engine counts it as moved. Rare.
- Double-KO promotion order after an attack (was #1): the attacker (turn player) promotes first, whoever went first — Dustin from experience; the one frame-by-frame case (225430) agrees. Engine fixed in rules1 (attacker first in either seat); 225430 replays correctly in rules2 and rules3. For Checkup double KOs see #21.
- Knock Outs from removing an HP bonus (was #10), and from Ability damage: scored at once (game over on the winning point), promotion if it was Active, and the turn continues so you can still attack; a Benched KO scores with no promotion — Dustin + 025604. Engine ✓.
- Two Nihilego (was #14): they stack — +10 Poison each ("Your opponent's Active Pokémon takes +10 damage from being Poisoned.") — Dustin, certain. Engine ✓ (+10 per opponent's Nihilego). Toxapex's Severe Poison with Nihilego: 40, +10 per Nihilego, stacking (two benched Nihilego: 60) — **Dustin tested it (2026-09-25)**; engine ✓, and his test confirms brews 03a/03b. Still open: whether a later ordinary Poison resets it to 10 (the engine resets it).
- Regigigas (Seal of Antiquity) with Alolan Muk (Power of Alchemy) in play: it can attack (the point of Dustin's deck 01); Muk also removes the opponent's Basic Pokémon Abilities. [DUSTIN 2026-09-25]; engine ✓ (tests in `regigigas_seal_of_antiquity_test.rs`).
- Evolving after using a once-per-turn Ability (was #13): moot — no card has an activated Ability on both stages. The only pair, Drizzile → Inteleon (both "Swift Shot"), triggers when that card is played to evolve, and one Pokémon can't evolve twice in a turn.
- v1.7.0 rule change (was #18): named-card searches (Clemont, Gladion, Team Galactic Grunt) can be played even when every named card is visibly out of the deck; still blocked with an empty deck. JP blog quoting the notice + Dustin's Gladion test (recording 152812, 04:27–04:30). No source lists any other card changed in 1.7.0. Seen in play for Gladion only; Clemont and Grunt rest on the Japanese source. Engine: Gladion and Grunt wrong; the Gladion fix must keep the empty play counting as the turn's Supporter. Versions since: 1.7.1 (Aug 5–6) and 1.7.2 (Sep 3–4) shipped as "bug fixes" with no itemized notes anywhere public (checked 2026-09-22).
- Wallace (was #16): usable on your first turn and on a Pokémon benched this turn — neither limit applies because the card doesn't print them (Rare Candy and Quick-Grow Extract do); the first turn is seen in recording 150630 (turn 1, the game asks "Please choose a Pokémon to evolve" with both the Active Magikarp and the Benched Carvanha selectable); Dustin is almost positive on the second. It can't pick a 50-HP Pokémon wearing Giant Cape (maximum HP 70) [OFFICIAL + DUSTIN]; damage doesn't change maximum HP. Engine: timing ✓; reads printed HP ✗ (on Astra's list).
- Searched cards (was #17): the opponent's Poké Ball result goes to hand **face-down** — seen frame by frame in 220914 and 025604 (plus review 170229). Dustin: any card going from deck to hand is hidden, Lisia included.
- Turn counter (was #7): counts both players' turns ("Current turn: 6" right after turn 5, Dustin's, in 225430; "Current turn: 2" (opponent) then "3" (Dustin) in 220914).
- Paralysis (was #3): "After its owner's next turn, it recovers during Pokémon Checkup." — in-app Tips.
- Confused Tails (was #15): "the attack doesn't happen and the player's turn ends" — none of the attack, including costs or effects, happens — in-app Tips.
- Timer expiry (was #8): the player whose battle clock runs out **loses** — in-app Tips (Bulbapedia/Game8 EN were wrong).
- Evolution ends effects of attacks on that Pokémon (was #5): yes — in-app Tips: "When a Pokémon evolves, any effects of attacks … or Special Conditions affecting the Pokémon end."
- Energy types: up to 3 (in-game text). Older guide said 2 — outdated. Energy Zone randomness with 2–3 types: equal odds is the stated design (JP guides); no one has published a test.
- Coin flips: a community tally of several hundred flips came out ~48.4% heads, within normal variance [COMMUNITY-TESTED, JP].
- First player draws on turn 1: yes (four recordings) — Game8 EN is wrong.
- Deck-out is not a loss: yes — Game8 EN is wrong. Seen again 2026-09-22 (Astra's review of 022627: "No cards left in deck; unable to draw cards" three turns running, game continued).
- Unused Energy: discarded at end of turn (Bulbapedia, pokemon-zone).
- Retaliation vs an attack that removes Abilities (was part of the Itchy Pollen note): settled by the official JP FAQ — Budew's Prickly Powder stops Druddigon's Rough Skin on that same attack. The engine gets this wrong (`07` M4).
