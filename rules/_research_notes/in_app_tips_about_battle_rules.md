**About battle rules › About the zones used in battle** (screenshot 2026-09-22 07:59)
The various zones used in battle have the following names:
• Deck
The cards in the deck are placed face down here.
• Active Spot
This is the spot where Pokémon use attacks to do battle. The Pokémon in the Active Spot is called the Active Pokémon.
• Bench
This is where Pokémon that can switch with the Active Pokémon are placed. The Pokémon on the Bench are called Benched
Pokémon.
• Discard pile
This is where Knocked Out Pokémon or cards that were used are placed. Cards placed here are said to be discarded. Cards
are always placed face up in the discard pile. Both players in a battle can see how many cards are in either discard
pile, and they can look at all the cards in either discard pile at any time.
• Hand
In a battle, these are the cards players use. A player can only have up to 10 cards in their hand.

**Setting up to play** (screenshot 2026-09-22 05:25; the heading itself is just above the visible area)
Setting up to play happens in this order:
1. A coin will be flipped automatically. If a player gets heads, they go first, and if they get tails, they go second.
2. Cards will be drawn from the deck automatically.
3. Players select a Basic Pokémon to put in the Active Spot. Players can also select Basic Pokémon to place in open spots
   on their Bench. (At this stage, players can't see which Pokémon their opponent has put in play.)
4. Players tap Start Battle to finish setting up to play.
5. When both players have finished setting up to play, the Pokémon they've put into play are turned face up, and the
   battle begins.

Notes:
• Players that go over the time limit for setting up to play will have a random Basic Pokémon placed in their Active
Spot, and the battle will begin.
• If a player taps Reset before they're done setting up to play, the Pokémon they've put into play will be returned to
their hand.

**Parts of a turn**
**The start of a turn**
When your turn starts, you draw a card from your deck and put it in your hand. After that, Energy will be automatically
generated in your Energy Zone.
The Energy Zone will display the Energy that was just generated and the Energy that will be generated next.
Note: On the first turn of the player who goes first, no Energy will be generated in the Energy Zone.

**Put Pokémon on the Bench**
If you have Basic Pokémon in your hand, you can put them onto your Bench. There are three spots on the Bench, and if any
spots are open, you can put as many Basic Pokémon as you want onto your Bench at any time during your turn.

**Attach Energy to Pokémon**
You have to attach Energy to Pokémon if you want to use their attacks. As long as there is Energy in your Energy Zone,
you can attach as much of that Energy as you'd like to Pokémon during your own turn. You are free to attach the Energy
to either your Active Pokémon or Benched Pokémon. You can attach Energy of [one type to Pokémon of a different type —

**Play Trainer cards**
If you have Trainer cards in your hand, you can play them. After you play them, they are discarded.
Note: You can play only one Supporter card per turn.

**Evolve your Pokémon**
If you have any Evolution Pokémon in your hand that evolve from your Pokémon in play you can play them from your hand to
evolve a Pokémon. You play the Evolution Pokémon on top of the Pokémon it evolves from.
You can't evolve a Pokémon on its first turn in play.
Notes:
• Neither player can evolve Pokémon on the first turn, whether they go first or second.
• When a Pokémon evolves, any effects of attacks (e.g. "on the next turn, attacks do more damage") or Special Conditions
affecting the Pokémon end.

**Use Pokémon Abilities**
Some Pokémon cards have Abilities along with their attacks.
If you tap on one of your Pokémon in play that has an Ability, and then select the Ability you want to use, you can
apply its effect.
Some Abilities work all the time automatically even without you using them as long as the Pokémon is in play. Also, some
Abilities work only if certain conditions are met.

**Retreat**
You can retreat your Active Pokémon and switch it with a Pokémon from your Bench.
To retreat, you must discard 1 Energy from your Active Pokémon for each Colorless Energy symbol listed in its Retreat
Cost. The discarded Energy can be of any type.
After your Active Pokémon retreats, choose one of your Benched Pokémon and put it in the Active Spot.
Notes:
• You can only retreat once per turn.
• You can't retreat if you can't discard enough Energy from the Active Pokémon, or if you don't have any Benched Pokémon.
• After retreating, the Pokémon keeps any damage it has taken, as well as all remaining attached Energy.
• When the retreating Pokémon returns to your Bench, any effects of attacks (e.g. "on the next turn, attacks do more
damage") or Special Conditions affecting the Pokémon end.

**Use your Pokémon's attacks**
You can use one of your Active Pokémon's attacks to attack your opponent's Active Pokémon.
Only the Active Pokémon can use attacks.
You can't use an attack if you don't have the required Energy to use that attack attached to the Pokémon.
If there are Energy symbols of a specific type in the required Energy, that type of Energy is required. If there are
Colorless Energy symbols in the required Energy, any type of Energy can be used.
Unless the attack text says otherwise, the Energy remains attached to the Pokémon after it uses its attack.
Don't apply Weakness for Benched Pokémon.
After you attack, your turn ends.

**Knocking Pokémon Out**
Players that Knock Out an opponent's Pokémon get one point. If a player Knocks Out more than one Pokémon at the same
time, they get a point for each Pokémon.
The player whose Pokémon was Knocked Out discards the Pokémon, as well as all Energy and other cards attached to it.
After that, the player puts one of their Benched Pokémon in the Active Spot.
Note: Most of the time, players only get one point when they Knock Out a Pokémon, but they may get two or more points if
they Knock Out Pokémon with a Rule Box.

**Winning or losing a battle**
Victory and defeat in a battle are determined if even one of the following conditions is met:
• If one player gets the set number of points for that battle or more before the other player, that player wins the
battle.
• If a player doesn't have any Pokémon remaining in play, that player loses the battle regardless of the number of
points each player has.
Notes:
• If a player decides to concede, that player loses the battle regardless of the state of cards in play or the number
of points each player has.
• A player will not lose even if they run out of cards in their deck.

**Pokémon Checkup and Special Conditions**
*What is Pokémon Checkup?*
Pokémon Checkup is a step that happens after the end of each turn. During Pokémon Checkup, the state of both players'
Pokémon is checked.
If there are any Abilities or Trainer cards that come into effect during Pokémon Checkup, those effects are applied.
Any Pokémon that has no HP remaining at the end of Pokémon Checkup is Knocked Out.
If both players' Pokémon are affected by Special Conditions, the player whose turn just ended checks their Special
Conditions first.
If one Pokémon is affected by multiple Special Conditions, Special Conditions are checked in the following order:
1. Poisoned
2. Burned
3. Asleep
4. Paralyzed
Note: Pokémon Checkup is not considered to be part of either player's turn.

*Special Conditions*
Special Conditions are conditions that are applied to Active Pokémon by attacks or other means. Poisoned, Burned,
Asleep, Paralyzed, and Confused are Special Conditions.
• Poisoned Pokémon take 10 damage during each Pokémon Checkup. Poisoned can stack with other Special Conditions.
• Burned Pokémon take 20 damage during each Pokémon Checkup. The player whose Pokémon is Burned flips a coin during each
Pokémon Checkup, and if heads, the Pokémon recovers from being Burned. If tails, the Pokémon stays Burned. Burned can
stack with other Special Conditions.
• Asleep Pokémon cannot attack or retreat. The player whose Pokémon is Asleep flips a coin during each Pokémon Checkup.
If heads, the Pokémon recovers. If tails, it stays Asleep.
• Paralyzed Pokémon cannot attack or retreat. After its owner's next turn, it recovers during Pokémon Checkup.
• When Confused Pokémon attack, the owner must flip a coin. If heads, the attack works normally, but if tails, the
attack doesn't happen and the player's turn ends.
Note: Asleep, Paralyzed, and Confused cannot stack with each other. If one of these Special Conditions is applied, it
replaces any of the others.

Pokémon recover from Special Conditions if one of the following conditions are met:
• The Pokémon moves to the Bench. Only Active Pokémon can have Special Conditions applied to them. If an Active Pokémon
returns to the Bench by retreating or some other way, it recovers from all Special Conditions.
• The Pokémon evolves. If the Pokémon evolves, it recovers from all Special Conditions.
• A card effect is used. Some Pokémon attacks and Abilities, and some Trainer cards, have effects that allow Pokémon to
recover from Special Conditions.

**Turn limits and time limits**
Battles have a limited number of turns. Once the turn limit is reached, the battle ends in a tie.
There are also time limits for each player. These time limits include the following:
• Battle time limits — Each player has a set time limit for each battle. A player who reaches their time limit loses
the battle. The timer will count down even on opponent turns if the game is waiting for your input.
• Time limits during your own turn — There is a time limit for how long you can play during each turn. A player who
reaches this time limit will have their turn forcibly ended, and their opponent's turn will begin. This timer resets on
each turn.
• Time limits during your opponent's turn — During your opponent's turn, there is a time limit when the game is waiting
for your input. If this time limit is reached, you will no longer be able to input commands, and the game will
automatically resolve the situation. This timer resets each time the game asks either player for input. Time limits
during Pokémon Checkup are handled in the same way.

**Communication errors during battles**
If your network connection loses strength during a solo battle, you'll be able to restore the battle after
reconnecting. You can also do this after closing the app and restarting it.
If your network connection loses strength during a versus battle, there's a certain amount of time (a few minutes)
during which you can reconnect. If you are able to reconnect, the battle can be restored. Time limit timers for the
battle will continue to count down during the time in which you can reconnect.
If your opponent's network connection loses strength, the game will wait for them to reconnect. If you are successfully
connected, and the battle cannot be restored during the time in which your opponent is able to reconnect, you win the
battle. On the other hand, if your network connection loses strength and you are not able to restore the battle during
the time in which you are able to reconnect, you lose the battle. If both players' network connections lose strength
and both players are disconnected, the battle ends and it is counted as a loss for both players.
